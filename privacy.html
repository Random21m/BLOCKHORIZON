PRIVACY

BLOCKHORIZON has no accounts, no analytics, no ads, no cookies and no trackers. The site is plain static files on GitHub Pages.

Your browser asks the public mempool.space API directly for block, fee and mempool data, and opens a live price stream straight to Kraken's public feed (ws.kraken.com); only while Kraken has sent no price for 15 seconds, it opens Coinbase's public price feed instead (ws-feed.exchange.coinbase.com, for US dollars, euros and pounds) and closes it as soon as Kraken is back; the trading chart on the Charts page also asks Kraken's public API (api.kraken.com) for past price candles, and its live feed for the order book and trades. Price alerts and the lines you draw are kept only in your browser. Each of them can see your IP address like any website you visit; we never receive it, and nothing is sent to them except the public data request.

Your settings, alarms and last-seen values are kept in this browser's local storage and never leave your device (except as described next). Clear your site data to remove them.

On your own node only (Umbrel, StartOS or the container), the Alarm page offers On your phone. It is off until you turn it on. Then this browser sends its alarms (heights, fee limits, prices and their messages) to your node, and your node sends each one, when it is reached, to the ntfy topic you chose (ntfy.sh unless you enter your own ntfy server). ntfy.sh can read those messages, and so can anyone who knows the topic's name, which is why it suggests a random one. Turning it off removes this browser's alarms from your node. The public site has no such feature.

You can choose another data server, such as your own node, under Settings, Data; from then on your browser asks that server instead of mempool.space. If the data server does not answer, your browser asks blockstream.info for the essentials (the block height, recent blocks, fees, the mempool and transaction lookups) until it is back; the connection dot turns amber while it does.

When you look up a transaction, its ID is sent to mempool.space to fetch it, so mempool.space could link that transaction to your IP address. If that matters to you, look up your own transactions on your own node instead. With Tell me when it is confirmed on, your browser asks about that transaction again at each new block until it is confirmed (and, with On your phone on, so does your node).

On the "Your block" page, the date and name you enter stay in the page address and in your browser. To find the block, the time of that day is sent to mempool.space (and, for the price that week, the date and your currency). Share the link only if you are happy for others to see the date.

On the "Your block" page, Share on Nostr does nothing until you press it. With a Nostr browser extension, the extension asks you to sign the note, and the signed note is sent to three public Nostr relays (relay.damus.io, nos.lol and relay.primal.net), where it is public like any Nostr post. Without one, the text is only copied to your clipboard.

If a website embeds the BLOCKHORIZON widget, your browser loads it from GitHub Pages and asks mempool.space for the block height (or price), just like the app. The widget stores nothing about you and the embedding site learns nothing from it.

On the Mining page, your browser also asks mempool.space for the transactions in the next block (over the same live connection) and for each mining pool's blocks of the past week. Nothing about you is sent. If you follow a transaction there, its ID is sent to mempool.space to check its status, as with any transaction lookup (see above), and it stays in the page address.

GitHub Pages, which hosts the files, may keep ordinary server logs under its own policy.
